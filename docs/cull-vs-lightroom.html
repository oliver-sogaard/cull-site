Culling in Lightroom, or before it. What each does well, and how the two fit together.

Lightroom can cull. Many photographers run the whole pass in the Library module with P, X and the arrows, and it works. The question is whether that is the best hour of the job or the slowest one. This is the comparison, point by point, with the handoff at the end.

Before anything is sharp

Lightroom renders its own previews, and until it has, a frame on screen is soft and the loupe lags behind the key. On a few thousand RAW files the wait runs from minutes to the better part of an hour, depending on the computer and the preview size you asked for. CULL reads the full-size JPEG the camera already wrote inside each file, so the folder is on screen in seconds and the first frame is sharp when you reach it.

The keys

In Lightroom: P picks, X rejects, U clears, the arrows move, and Caps Lock turns on auto-advance so a verdict also moves to the next frame. Z zooms to the point you click. In CULL: Enter keeps, Backspace rejects, F marks a favorite, the arrows move, and every verdict advances. Hold Space and the frame is at 100 % at the point the camera focused; let go and you are back. Both are fast once learned. The difference is that CULL's keys are the whole app, with nothing between the hand and the next frame.

Twins and bursts

Lightroom's filmstrip is flat. Stacking by capture time exists, but it is a menu away and the stacks still open one at a time. CULL groups frames of one scene before you reach them and boxes a burst as one, so the choice is always "which of these" rather than "is this one any good". That is where most of the time in a cull is won.

The catalog

Culling in Lightroom means importing everything first. Every frame becomes a catalog row with previews on disk, including the ones you will reject in the next minute. Culling before import means only the keeps are ever imported, and the catalog stays the size of your work.

What Lightroom does better

Everything after the cull. Editing, stars for finished work, collections, export, the catalog itself. CULL stops where that begins. It writes a keep as Lightroom's pick flag, a reject as its reject flag and a favorite as a pick with a purple label, into an XMP sidecar beside the file, so Lightroom reads the verdicts on import as if you had made them there. The image data is never touched.

Other culling tools

Photo Mechanic is the long-standing choice of news and sports photographers, and it works on the same principle: the embedded JPEG, the keys, the sidecar. Narrative Select, FilterPixel, Aftershoot and Imagen cull with a model that scores the frames for focus, eyes and duplicates, so part of the decision is made for you. CULL keeps the first idea and none of the second. The decision stays yours, and the app's one job is to make it take one key.

The handoff

  • Cull the folder in CULL. Close it when every frame is decided.
  • In Lightroom, import the folder with Add, not Copy, so the files stay where they are and the sidecars beside them are read.
  • Filter on Flagged. Those are the keeps; the purple ones are the favorites. The rejects are flagged as rejects and can be left out of the import or deleted from the catalog in one step.
  • If the folder was already in the catalog, select the photos and choose Metadata → Read Metadata from Files.
